\# UniEase — UX Research Plan



\*\*Project:\*\* UniEase — Smart Academic Success Platform  

\*\*Research phase:\*\* Discovery  

\*\*Status:\*\* Planned — research has not yet been conducted



\## 1. Research Background



UniEase is a proposed academic success platform for university students across different disciplines.



The application aims to help students organise academic responsibilities, manage deadlines, set academic and career goals, monitor progress, and receive AI-assisted study guidance.



Before developing these features, we must investigate students' actual experiences, needs, and difficulties.



\## 2. Research Problem



University students may experience difficulties managing academic tasks, planning study time, prioritising deadlines, and tracking progress towards their goals.



These are research hypotheses, not validated findings.



\## 3. Research Objectives



1\. Understand how students currently manage coursework and deadlines.

2\. Identify difficulties with existing academic planning tools.

3\. Investigate how students set academic and career goals.

4\. Discover which progress indicators students find meaningful.

5\. Explore expectations and concerns regarding AI study assistants.

6\. Identify accessibility and usability requirements across devices.

7\. Compare experiences across different academic disciplines.



\## 4. Target Participants



University students from different courses and academic years.



Potential disciplines include:



\- Computing and Software Engineering

\- Business and Management

\- Engineering

\- Science

\- Other university programmes



We will recruit participants from more than one discipline where feasible.



\## 5. Research Methods



\### Semi-Structured Interviews



\*\*Target:\*\* 6–8 students  

\*\*Duration:\*\* Approximately 15–20 minutes per participant



Interviews will explore real experiences, behaviours, challenges, and current planning methods.



\### Online Survey



\*\*Target:\*\* 20–30 responses



The survey will investigate academic planning habits, common challenges, device preferences, goal-setting behaviours, and expectations of digital tools.



These sample sizes are practical project targets and are not intended to represent the entire university student population.



\## 6. Key Research Questions



1\. How do students currently organise academic responsibilities?

2\. What causes difficulties when managing multiple deadlines?

3\. How do students decide what to study or complete first?

4\. How do students set and monitor academic and career goals?

5\. What information helps students recognise their progress?

6\. How do students currently use AI tools for academic support?

7\. What concerns do students have about privacy and AI-generated advice?

8\. How do planning needs differ across courses and academic years?



\## 7. Research Ethics



\- Explain the research purpose before participation.

\- Obtain informed, voluntary consent.

\- Allow participants to skip questions or withdraw.

\- Request separate permission before recording interviews.

\- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.

\- Anonymise research notes before including them in the project.

\- Keep identifiable responses and recordings outside the public GitHub repository.



\## 8. Research Analysis



We will:



1\. Organise anonymised interview notes.

2\. Identify repeated behaviours, challenges, and unmet needs.

3\. Group findings using affinity mapping.

4\. Compare interview findings with survey patterns.

5\. Identify differences between academic disciplines.

6\. Prioritise user needs based on the collected evidence.

7\. Develop evidence-based personas and user journeys.



\## 9. Expected Deliverables



\- Interview guide

\- Survey questionnaire

\- Anonymised research findings

\- Affinity map

\- User personas

\- Student journey maps

\- Prioritised UX requirements

\- Research limitations



\## 10. Research Success Criteria



Research will be considered ready for synthesis when we have documented participants' experiences, identified recurring problems, considered contradictory evidence, and recorded limitations.



The quality of the findings matters more than achieving a specific response count.



\## 11. Current Research Status

No interviews, survey responses, findings, personas, or usability test results are claimed at this stage.
