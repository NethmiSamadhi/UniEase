\# UniEase — Survey Findings



\## 1. Research Overview



The survey explored how students organise academic work, track

progress, set goals, and use AI for studying.



Source: UniEase University Student Academic Experience Survey CSV.

Analysis date: 24 October 2026.



\- Total responses: 20

\- Consenting responses analysed: 19

\- Responses excluded because consent was declined: 1



All percentages below use 19 consenting respondents unless stated

otherwise. Results describe this sample, not all university students.

\## 3. Key Findings



\### Deadline Management



\- 14 respondents sometimes struggle with multiple deadlines.

\- 3 respondents often struggle.

\- 2 respondents rarely struggle.

\- Combined, 17 of 19 respondents (89.5%) struggle sometimes or often.



\### Main Study Challenges



| Challenge | Respondents |

| --- | ---: |

| Deciding which tasks to prioritise | 7 |

| Balancing studies and personal responsibilities | 4 |

| Staying motivated | 3 |

| Tracking academic progress | 2 |

| Managing multiple assignments | 1 |

| Remembering deadlines | 1 |

| Planning study time | 1 |



Prioritisation was the most selected challenge, but it was not

selected by a majority.



\### Task Selection



8 respondents (42.1%) usually choose the task they feel most

motivated to complete. Only 3 reported following a study schedule.

\## 4. Data Quality and Limitations



\- The sample is small and recruitment representativeness is unknown.

\- Responses are self-reported.

\- Two respondents selected AI assistance options together with

&#x20; "I would not use AI academic assistance".

\- Some progress-tracking responses combine tracking methods with

&#x20; "I do not currently track my progress".

\- Conflicting selections must not be treated as unambiguous evidence.

\- One respondent left the AI-concerns question blank.

\- All 19 consenting respondents left the final improvement question blank.

\- No qualitative themes can be inferred from those blank answers.



\## 5. Proposed Design Decisions



These are design hypotheses to test, not proven solutions.



| Evidence | Proposed response |

| --- | --- |

| Frequent deadline difficulties | Prominent upcoming deadlines and assignment status |

| Prioritisation is the most selected challenge | Sorting by deadline and user-selected priority |

| Difficulty balancing responsibilities | Flexible calendar and study scheduling |

| Interest in study consistency | Simple weekly study summary |

| Interest in goals and assignment progress | Milestones and completion summaries |

| Laptop and desktop use is common | Desktop wireframes followed by mobile adaptations |

| AI dependency and cost concerns | Optional assistant with clear capabilities and limits |

\## 7. Usability Validation



Ask students to complete these tasks using the wireframes:



1\. Add an assignment with a module and deadline.

2\. Find the nearest upcoming deadline.

3\. Mark an assignment as completed.

4\. Schedule a study session.



Record task completion, errors, requests for help, and feedback.

Revise the designs based on observed problems.
